Priority Guardian — Product Briefing
What It Is
A ChatGPT-based self-accountability tool that makes time allocation visible against explicitly declared priorities. It reads your Google Calendar, matches events to your stated priorities, and shows you the gap between intention and reality — weekly, with data, no interpretation.
Who It's For
Freelancers, solopreneurs, and independent professionals who juggle multiple priorities without a team or manager to keep them honest. People who stay busy but lose sight of what actually matters.
Core Problem
Smart, self-directed people don't lack ambition — they lack visibility. They declare priorities but have no feedback loop showing whether their calendar reflects those priorities. By the time they notice the drift, weeks have passed.
How It Works
Set up: Connect Google Calendar (read-only). Define up to 3 priorities — deadline-driven or flexible. Set time targets and detection keywords.
Work: Schedule priority work into your calendar like you normally would. Priority Guardian watches, not controls.
Review: Weekly data snapshot compares declared time targets against actual tagged calendar time. Pure numbers: intended hours, observed hours, delta.
Reflect (optional): Mini retrospective with the LLM — what got in the way, what to change.
What It Is NOT
Not a calendar app
Not a task manager
Not a coach that tells you what to do
Not an AI that interprets, evaluates, or recommends
It's a mirror. It shows you the data. You decide what it means.
Value Proposition
"See where your time actually goes — against what you said matters."
The weekly snapshot creates a lightweight accountability loop that doesn't require willpower, a team, or a manager. The constraint (max 3 priorities) forces clarity. The data forces honesty.
Key Design Principles
Behavioral feedback, not performance evaluation. No "you're behind" narratives. Just declared vs. observed.
Calendar as source of truth. No manual time tracking. If it's not on your calendar, it didn't happen.
Conversational onboarding. No forms, no widgets for setup. The LLM collects configuration through conversation.
User controls the cycle. Reports on demand. Configuration changes anytime. The recurring review event is a suggestion, not a requirement.
